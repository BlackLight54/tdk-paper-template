\chapter{The TDK lifecycle}\label{app:lifecycle}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Plan the phases in \cref{tab:lifecycle} backwards from the official dates in \cref{app:tdk}. Each step has an owner, a completion condition and a link to guidance.
We use these steps to track each student's progress at the weekly consultation (\cref{app:consult}).
Set \texttt{\textbackslash lintphase} in \texttt{thesis.tex} to your phase's name in lower case, from \texttt{topic} to \texttt{submit}; the lint's checks come due by phase (\cref{app:code:paperci}). After the upload, leave it at \texttt{submit}: the paper is submitted and needs no further checks.
Inspect the reviewer PDF every week. It is the version reviewers will read.

Three steps are \anchor{}s: the topic manifest, the spine and the full draft. An anchor is an artefact we approve before the next phase, and later work must follow it. The topic manifest and the spine are one page each; we approve the full draft at a revision recorded in the consultation summary. The topic starts as a direction. The spine fixes its claims and names; the full draft leaves only polishing. You can continue working at an anchor, but work that contradicts it is drift. Revisit the agreement first, or you may have to redo everything built on it.

Red rows mark anchors. Yellow rows marked \official{} are faculty steps with fixed portal dates (\cref{app:tdk:dates}); missing one ends that semester's TDK. Green rows marked \department{} are department sessions for all its TDK students. We organise them to resolve the final problems together.

\section{When to run reviewer passes}\label{app:lifecycle:when-passes}
Run the reviewer passes (\cref{app:lifecycle:reviewers}) at four steps of \cref{tab:lifecycle}: the premortem, the panel pass, the skim pass, and the reviews and questions. Each row says when the step is done.

\section{Requirements}\label{app:lifecycle:polish}
The words \emph{must}, \emph{we recommend} and \emph{you can} mean what \cref{app:here:words} says.

\Cref{tab:polish} lists the requirements, in the order they come due. A rule name marks a row the lint checks; \emph{reading} marks a row you check yourself. The lint's rows are checked by \texttt{make check-reviewer}, which runs every rule against the reviewer PDF (\cref{app:code:paperci}); keep them passing before the last week, as the paper CI runs them on every push. Use the polish phase for checks. Any row that takes more than an hour should have been completed in the draft phase. Before upload, set \texttt{\textbackslash lintphase} to \texttt{submit} and go through every row due by \texttt{submit}.
