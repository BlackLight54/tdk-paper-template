\chapter{Weekly consultations}\label{app:consult}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Find a recurring slot.} Agree on a weekly time slot with us and put it in as a recurring MS Teams event, so it does not have to be negotiated every week.

\paragraph{Why every week.} A week is short enough that a wrong turn costs days, not a month, and long enough to have something to show. Come even when little moved: a blocked week is exactly what we can help with.

\paragraph{Why one-on-one.} A group meeting averages over everyone's problem, and yours is specific: your code, your draft, your plot. One-on-one we read your actual artefact and ask back until the misunderstanding is found, which takes minutes alone and never happens in a group. It also lets us be frank about scope and effort, which is not a group conversation.

\paragraph{Why in person.} In a room we share a whiteboard, which is where notation, architecture and the leading figure actually get drawn. We see where you hesitate, which a call hides. The hard conversations, when a topic has to be cut or re-aimed, go better face to face. Hold the Teams call anyway, for the recording and the transcript (see below).

\paragraph{Come with a presentation.} If you arrive with a few slides on what you did this week, or slides that make your work easier for us to understand, we will very much appreciate it, even if an LLM generated them. A good default:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item what you did since the last consultation, with evidence: a figure, a result, a page of text;
  \item what you read: the papers you added to Zotero this week, one sentence each on why they matter;
  \item what is blocked or where you are unsure;
  \item what you plan for next week;
  \item the questions you need us to decide.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Close with a summary.} At the end of each consultation, say back in your own words what we agreed: the decisions, your next steps, and what we owe you. Then post the same as a short written summary in \texttt{notes/} of your notes repository. If your summary differs from what we meant, we catch it now, not in a week.

\paragraph{The mirror principle.} We put in what you put in, and we are open about it. A student who works hard on their topic gets a lot of our time, our contacts and our help beyond the paper; a student who has put the topic on the back burner gets less of it. This does not mean that a student who struggles is left behind: it means that we are mindful of where our time goes, and that you decide how much of it you get. On a separate matter, we value consistency over bursts. A student who is diligent every week and not very productive gets further than one who disappears for a month and then does brilliant work for a weekend, because research compounds and a weekly rhythm is what keeps the topic alive.

\paragraph{Recordings and Teams calls.} Hold the consultation as a Teams call, even if we sit in one room, and record it with transcription. You can then rewatch the details, feed the transcript to your AI tools, and turn it into notes in your notes repository. For the transcript, we recommend \href{https://github.com/openai/whisper}{Whisper} large-v3-turbo over the built-in Teams transcription: it is more accurate and handles multilingual speech better, and we usually speak a mix of Hungarian and English. Ask us for our transcription skill.
